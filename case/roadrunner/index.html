Road Runner

Most websites collect leads. This one queries the business behind it.

The problem

A company's systems each know something the customer needs, and none of them can be reached from the only place the customer actually goes. The CRM knows the account, the inventory system knows the stock, the finance system knows the trading history — and the website, the front door, knows none of it. So every enquiry starts from zero, is answered by a human reading three screens, and arrives in the CRM as an orphan lead with no relationship attached.

What was built

  • A front door that identifies the visitor and branches — one entry point, a genuinely different engagement per audience.
  • Company-name resolution against the CRM, so an enquiry attaches to the account that will fulfil it instead of creating another duplicate.
  • A read layer into inventory and finance, so stock, lead times and account-tier pricing can be answered on the page.
  • Per-client and per-campaign landing pages with UTM tagging carried end to end, so engagement lands back on the same record.
  • Reporting built on the unified record rather than on each silo's own version of the truth.

What changed

  • 170 per-client landing pages live and tracked, up from 150 three weeks earlier — each one generated in minutes rather than queued behind a developer.
  • 229 stonemasons mapped to the businesses they actually work with. Before the reach view existed, only 20 had any recorded relationship at all.
  • Enquiries arrive segmented, attributed and attached to an account, so the qualification happens before a human reads them.
  • The CRM ends up restructured as a by-product: branching logic forces a real taxonomy of who the business sells to.
  • Built in 109 hours across May and June 2026, site and platform together.

Method

How software gets built this fast without being rubbish.

Stage 1

Specification before generation

The commercial logic is written down first — the object model, the states, the acceptance criteria. AI is fast at producing code and hopeless at deciding what should be true.

Stage 2

Directed build, in the loop

AI engineering agents do the typing under direction, in small verifiable increments. Twenty-five years of knowing what a working revenue system looks like is what makes the direction worth anything.

Stage 3

Ground-truth gates

Nothing advances on a claim. Each increment is checked against real data and a real user's workflow before the next one starts. This is the whole difference between a demo and a production tenant.

Stage 4

Commercial close

Priced, packaged, onboarded and measured. A build that never gets a revenue-per-customer number attached to it isn't finished, it's abandoned.

Why this is the hard part

Anyone can log page views. The value is the decision.

Tracking is table stakes and nobody buys it. What's actually difficult is the layer above: a score that reflects how you qualify, a stage your sales team recognises, and a next action worded so specifically that a rep does it instead of ignoring it.

Notice the score is interrogable — you can see exactly which of your actions moved it. Sales teams act on a score they can argue with, and quietly ignore one they can't.
