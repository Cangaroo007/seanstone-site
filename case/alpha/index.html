Alpha Surfaces inquiry engine

One form. Four audiences. Four completely different conversations.

The problem

A stone supplier's website had one contact form for four completely different customers. A homeowner wanting a sample, a stonemason checking stock, an architect needing specification data and a builder chasing lead times all arrived as the same undifferentiated enquiry, into the same inbox, to be sorted by hand. The account relationships that already existed in the CRM were invisible at the moment they mattered most.

What was built

  • A self-segmenting enquiry path: the visitor declares what they are and the form reconfigures around them.
  • Company-name lookup against the CRM at the point of enquiry, linking to the existing account and its trading history.
  • Four distinct journeys — sample requests, trade pricing, specification packs, lead-time answers — off a single entry point.
  • Routing rules that send each type to the right team with the right priority.

What changed

  • Enquiries arrive pre-sorted and attached to the right account.
  • The specification path makes architect interest visible months before the order it eventually produces.

Method

How software gets built this fast without being rubbish.

Stage 1

Specification before generation

The commercial logic is written down first — the object model, the states, the acceptance criteria. AI is fast at producing code and hopeless at deciding what should be true.

Stage 2

Directed build, in the loop

AI engineering agents do the typing under direction, in small verifiable increments. Twenty-five years of knowing what a working revenue system looks like is what makes the direction worth anything.

Stage 3

Ground-truth gates

Nothing advances on a claim. Each increment is checked against real data and a real user's workflow before the next one starts. This is the whole difference between a demo and a production tenant.

Stage 4

Commercial close

Priced, packaged, onboarded and measured. A build that never gets a revenue-per-customer number attached to it isn't finished, it's abandoned.

Why this is the hard part

Anyone can log page views. The value is the decision.

Tracking is table stakes and nobody buys it. What's actually difficult is the layer above: a score that reflects how you qualify, a stage your sales team recognises, and a next action worded so specifically that a rep does it instead of ignoring it.

Notice the score is interrogable — you can see exactly which of your actions moved it. Sales teams act on a score they can argue with, and quietly ignore one they can't.
